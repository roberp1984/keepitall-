# Claim deadlines by country: research for verification

EU261 sets no time limit: each country's national law decides (CJEU C-139/11 *Cuadrench Moré*). These periods were researched in October 2026. **Shown on the site since 27 October 2026 (☑):** England and Wales, Scotland, Germany, France, Spain, Poland, Belgium, Norway and Sweden (with a warning to notify the airline within 2 months). The rest stay hidden.

How the site uses them: it picks the departure country (or the arrival country if that one has a verified period; UK airports map to England and Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland). The result screen says *"You have until DD Month YYYY to bring a claim in [country]"* and warns when fewer than 180 days are left. A period that is **too long** could make someone miss their deadline, so only enable a country when you are confident.

**Recommendation:** enable the 8 marked *high* (except Sweden, which needs an extra warning first). Ask a lawyer about *medium* ones. Keep *low* ones hidden.

| ☐ | Ireland | 6 years | flight date | medium | [link](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/travel-and-recreation/air-travel/compensation-for-overbooked-and-delayed-flights/) | Six years for contract claims (Statute of Limitations 1957). | ☑ | Germany | 3 years | 31 Dec of the flight year | high | [link](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.de/wissen/reise-mobilitaet/unterwegs-sein/faq-zu-fluggastrechten-alles-wichtige-rund-ums-thema-39125) | Verbraucherzentrale: regular 3-year period from the end of the year of the flight (BGB §§195, 199); BGH X ZR 62/23 confirms it also for package holidays. |
| ☑ | France | 5 years | flight date | high | [link](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/particuliers/voyager-et-se-deplacer/vol-annule-ou-retarde-quels-sont-vos-droits) | French government: five years from the incident (Code civil art. 2224), confirmed by Cour de cassation, 10 Oct 2019 (no. 18-20.491). A DGCCRF page still mentions 2 years for delays (Montreal); the Cour de cassation applies 5. |
| ☑ | Spain | 5 years | flight date | high | [link](https://www.seguridadaerea.gob.es/en/preguntas-frecuentes-derechos-pasajeros) | AESA: five years from the flight date to claim from the airline; then one year from that claim to go to AESA's ADR. |
| ☐ | Italy | unsettled | — | low | [link](https://giuricivile.it/prescrizione-compensazione-pecuniaria-trasporto-aereo/) | Unsettled. Cassazione ord. 4427/2024 excluded the 2-year Montreal period but didn't set one; courts apply 6 months (Codice della navigazione art. 418), 1 year (c.c. art. 2951) or 10 years (c.c. art. 2946). Do not display; tell Italian claimants to act fast. |
| ☐ | Portugal | 3 years | flight date | low | [link](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/jcms/P_93813/it) | No official Portuguese source found. Three years (Código Civil art. 498) is reported in secondary sources only. |
| ☐ | Netherlands | unsettled | — | low | [link](https://www.eccnederland.nl/en/check-your-rights/travelling-eu/what-are-my-rights-if-my-flight-delayed) | Unsettled in the sources found: 2 years (Montreal by analogy) vs 5 years (BW art. 3:307). Do not display without legal advice. |
| ☑ | Belgium | 1 years | flight date | high | [link](https://www.jubel.be/fr/annulation-ou-retard-important-de-votre-vol-vous-devez-introduire-votre-action-dans-lannee/) | One year for actions under a passenger transport contract (Code de droit économique); the shortest in Europe. A 2022 bill to extend it to 3 years was not found to be adopted. A claim to the airline does not necessarily stop the clock. |
| ☐ | Austria | 3 years | flight date | medium | [link](https://europakonsument.at/) | Three years (ABGB §1489) per general sources; no OGH decision or official Austrian statement found. |
| ☑ | Poland | 1 years | flight date | high | [link](https://czasopismo.legeartis.org/2017/03/przedawnienie-odszkodowania-odwolany-lot/) | Polish Supreme Court resolution III CZP 111/16 (17 March 2017): EU261 Art. 7 claims prescribe after one year (Kodeks cywilny art. 778). |
| ☐ | Denmark | 3 years | flight date | medium | [link](https://lex.dk/flypassagerers_rettigheder) | Three years under the general Limitation Act (forældelsesloven); only secondary sources found. |
| ☑ | Sweden | 10 years | flight date | high | [link](https://lagen.nu/dom/nja/2018s127) | Ten years (Preskriptionslagen 1981:130, applied by ARN). BUT the Supreme Court (NJA 2018 s. 127) requires notifying the airline within a reasonable time; within 2 months of the trip is always in time. Needs a notice warning before display. |
| ☑ | Norway | 3 years | flight date | high | [link](https://www.domstol.no/globalassets/upload/hret/avgjorelser/2024/juni/hr-2024-1200-a.pdf) | Supreme Court HR-2024-1200-A: the Limitation Act (foreldelsesloven) applies, i.e. the general 3-year period (§2), not the Aviation Act. |
| ☐ | Switzerland | unsettled | — | low | [link](https://www.bazl.admin.ch/de/fluggastrechte) | Unknown: no Swiss source on the period (10 years under OR art. 127 is only an assumption). Swiss courts also treat delay compensation differently. Do not display. |
